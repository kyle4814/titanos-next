Builders x quote follow-up

Quote follow up for builders: when to message, what to say and a worked example

A builder's quote is a big document sent to a nervous buyer, and silence afterwards is normal. The client is comparing two other quotes, waiting on finance or talking to a partner. This page gives you the follow-up sequence, the exact words for each step and an example of what one revived quote is worth.

By Kyle Deligny, Titanos, Brisbane. Last reviewed 28 November 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Why this is different for builders

Building quotes are different from a plumber's callout price. They run to tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars, take days to prepare, and sit with the owner for weeks. A silent quote is usually not a no. It is more often a decision the client has not made or a question they have not asked.

That changes the follow-up. You are not chasing a payment or pushing a sale. You are offering to answer the question that is holding things up, and giving a clean way to say not now. The wording below is built around that.

Five building situations and the follow-up each one needs

  1. The client is comparing three builders. Silence for a week is common. Your follow-up should not drop the price. Offer to walk through the inclusions and exclusions, because the cheapest quote often leaves things out and an owner comparing line by line will notice a clear scope.
  2. Waiting on finance or approval. A renovation depends on a bank or a council decision. Ask where they are in the process and say you will hold your programme slot for a stated period. That gives them a reason to reply and you a date to plan around.
  3. Price shock on an extension. The owner did not expect the number. A follow-up that offers to talk about staging the work or revisiting the scope keeps the conversation open without discounting.
  4. An owner-builder wanting a rough figure. These clients ask for an estimate and then disappear. Mark the document as an estimate, as the Quote-to-Cash Kit does, and follow up once to see if they want a firm quote.
  5. A repeat client or a referral. A past client who has asked for a quote on a second job deserves a call, not a template. Use the text only to book the call.

The follow-up sequence, with exact wording

These messages were written for this page. They are a suggested cadence from general practice, not a tested one, and the timings are yours to change. Send the first by text and the second by email so the quote document sits in front of them again.

Hi [first name], it's [your name] from [business]. Just checking the quote for [job] came through okay. Happy to walk you through any line if that would help.
Day 7 emailA week after the quote, subject: Your quote for [job]
Written for this page
Hi [first name], I wanted to check in on the quote I sent on [date] for [job]. If you are comparing builders, I am happy to go through what is included and what is not, so the quotes are easy to compare like for like. We can hold a start window in [month] for another [number] days. If the timing no longer works, just let me know and I will close the quote off. Kind regards, [your name] [business], ABN [ABN]
Day 14 textTwo weeks after the quote
Written for this page
Hi [first name], [your name] from [business]. I will leave the quote for [job] with you. If you want to go ahead or have questions, reply here or call me on [number]. If it is not for you, no problem at all.
If they say not nowReply by you
Written for this page
Thanks for letting me know, [first name]. I will keep the quote on file for [number] days. If your plans change, message me and we can update it.
  • Stop after the third message unless they reply. Three polite contacts over two weeks is firm without being a chase.
  • Never add a deadline you will not honour. A real start window is fine; a made-up expiry is the kind of pressure that costs you the job.
  • Keep the quote itself clear: scope, price, GST, inclusions and exclusions in plain words. Check your state's rules for written contracts and deposits above set values before relying on an emailed quote as a contract.

How to run it, in order

  1. List every quote sent in the last 90 days and mark each as won, lost, or silent. The silent group is your follow-up list.
  2. Pick a cadence you can keep, such as day 3 by text, day 7 by email and day 14 by text, and write it in your job sheet so it is not left to memory.
  3. Put the follow-up date on the quote record at the time you send the quote. In the Tradie Quote-to-Cash Kit, quotes live in a Google Sheet with Quotes, Jobs and Invoices tabs, which gives you one place to see which quotes are silent.
  4. Send the first message by hand for the first month. You will learn which lines get replies before you automate anything.
  5. Record the outcome of every follow-up in the sheet: replied, won, lost, or no reply after three.
  6. After 90 days, compare your own revival rate with the example. Replace the example numbers with yours.

Spam Act 2003: following up a quote the customer asked for

A follow-up about a quote the person asked you for is closely tied to their enquiry. The Spam Act 2003 still applies to commercial electronic messages, and the ACMA says a message needs consent, must identify the sender and must carry an unsubscribe option that is honoured within 5 working days.

Our Quote-to-Cash Kit's own compliance notes take the careful line: it sends only messages about an enquiry the customer made, a quote they asked for, a job they accepted and an invoice. If you add offers, newsletters or review requests, those need their own consent and an unsubscribe. The ACMA also says inferred consent does not automatically cover sending messages after someone has just bought something.

In practice: ask for the customer's phone and email on the enquiry form with a tick box, keep a record of when they gave consent, include your business name, ABN and an opt-out line in every follow-up, and stop if they ask. This is general information, not legal advice.

Primary sources: ACMA: Avoid sending spam (Spam Act 2003 rules); Spam Act 2003, legislation.gov.au; OAIC: Australian Privacy Principles.

Questions builders ask

How many times should a builder follow up a quote?

There is no measured Australian figure we can cite. A common-sense limit is three contacts over about two weeks, spread across text and email, then stop unless they reply.

Should I offer a discount in the follow-up?

No. Offer to explain the scope or stage the work. A discount in a follow-up tells the client the first price was soft.

Does the kit chase unaccepted quotes automatically?

No. The Quote-to-Cash Kit turns an enquiry into a draft quote, an accepted quote into a job, a finished job into an invoice and sends payment reminders. Follow-ups on unaccepted quotes are written by you, using the wording on this page, until you decide to automate them.

Is a follow-up text allowed under the Spam Act?

A follow-up limited to a quote the person requested, from an identified sender with an opt-out, is the careful approach. Promotions need consent.
