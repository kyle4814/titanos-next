Painters x review requests

Google review request text for painters: templates, timing and what Google allows

A painter's work is visible for years and discussed at barbecues, but most of it never reaches Google. The owner is happy, the scaffold is down, and nobody asks. This page gives you the text and email wording to ask every customer once, the timing that suits painting jobs, and the rules on what you may and may not do.

By Kyle Deligny, Titanos, Brisbane. Last reviewed 28 November 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Why this is different for painters

Painting jobs end cleanly. There is a clear last day, a walk around with the owner and a result they can see. That is the best moment to ask, because the customer is looking at the finished wall. For a plumber the job ends when the water stops; for a painter it ends on a day you can plan for.

There is also a trap. It is tempting to ask only the customers who seemed happy. Google's own policy rules out soliciting content that does not represent a genuine experience, and offering anything in return. The method here asks everyone the same way.

Five painting situations and how to ask in each

  1. A full exterior repaint, finished today. Ask on the final day after the walk-around, by text, when the result is in front of the customer. Keep it to one message with the review link and a private option.
  2. An interior job for a landlord or agent. The person who pays is not the person who lives there. Send the request to the person who dealt with you, and keep the wording about the work, not about the tenant.
  3. A job with a touch-up callback. Wait until after the touch-up. A request sent before a snag is fixed produces a worse review than the finished job deserves.
  4. A customer who raised a concern at handover. Do not skip them and do not push them. Send the same message as everyone else, and make sure the private feedback option is there. Then follow up personally using the service recovery wording.
  5. A weather-delayed job. Painting stops for rain. If the job ran long, a short thank-you for patience in the message goes down well, but keep the request itself the same as for every other customer.

  • Never offer a discount, a gift or a free touch-up in exchange for a review, or for changing or removing one.
  • Send one request and one reminder, then stop. Texts under about 300 characters are easier to read on a phone.

How to set it up, in order

  1. Get your Google review link. In your Business Profile, the share option gives a direct link to leave a review. The kit also includes a link builder from your Place ID.
  2. Ask for consent at the start of the job. Put a line on your quote or booking form asking whether they are happy to receive a text about the job, and a review request after, and keep the record of when they agreed.
  3. Choose the timing: the day the job finishes after any touch-ups, in daylight hours, not late at night.
  4. Use one set of wording for everyone. The Google Review Request Engine sends one request and one reminder from n8n by SMS through Twilio or by email, with a daily cap and a cooldown per customer.
  5. Set up the private feedback form and decide who reads it and how quickly you reply. A low private rating is a chance to fix a problem, not to hide one.
  6. Count monthly: jobs finished, requests sent, reviews received. Compare with the example.

Spam Act 2003: are review requests allowed?

A request for a review promotes your business, so the ACMA's rules for commercial electronic messages are the safe way to treat it. The ACMA says you need the recipient's consent, the message must identify the business that sent it and carry correct contact details, and it must include an unsubscribe option honoured within 5 working days.

Express consent is best: a form, a tick box or a conversation, with a record of who agreed, when and how. The ACMA notes you cannot send a message to ask for consent. It also says inferred consent is not as reliable, and that it does not cover sending messages after someone has just bought something. So ask at the start of the job, not after it.

Our Quote-to-Cash Kit's compliance notes say the same thing: review requests need their own consent and unsubscribe. This is general information, not legal advice.

Primary sources: ACMA: Avoid sending spam (Spam Act 2003 rules); Spam Act 2003, legislation.gov.au; Google Maps user contributed content policy.

Questions painters ask

Can a painter send a Google review request by text in Australia?

Yes, with care. Treat it as a commercial message: get consent when you take the booking, name your business, include an opt-out such as Reply STOP, and honour it within 5 working days.

Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Google's policy does not allow offering incentives in exchange for posting a review or for changing or removing a negative one.

Should I only ask happy customers?

No. Soliciting only happy customers does not represent a genuine range of experience. Send the same two links to everyone, a Google link and a private feedback option.

When is the best time to ask a painting customer?

On the day the job finishes, after any touch-ups, once the customer has seen the result. This is a judgement based on how painting jobs end, not a measured figure.
