Plumbers x invoice chasing

Chasing unpaid invoices as a plumber: polite messages at 7, 14 and 30 days

Plumbers do the work first and get paid later, so an unpaid invoice is your own cash sitting in someone else's account. Most late payers are not refusing; the invoice went to the wrong inbox, or it is waiting in an approval queue. This page gives you the exact words for three reminders and an example of what faster payment is worth.

By Kyle Deligny, Titanos, Brisbane. Last reviewed 28 November 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Why this is different for plumbers

A plumbing invoice goes to many kinds of payer: a homeowner who meant to transfer tonight, a property manager whose approval queue takes a fortnight, or a strata manager who pays on a meeting cycle. Each of them fails to pay for a different reason, so a good reminder is neutral, asks a question and makes paying easy.

There is also a plumbing-specific leak: after-hours callouts done for a flat figure and never written up as a job, and invoices left until Sunday night. The best chaser is an invoice sent on the day the job finishes. The reminders below start from there.

Five plumbing payers and how the reminder should differ

  1. A homeowner who said they would transfer tonight. Give it a few days, then a friendly reminder with the amount, the due date and your bank details. Most will pay. Keep the tone warm; this person may need you again.
  2. A property manager with an approval queue. The invoice often sits with an accounts team who never saw your first email. Ask who in accounts should receive it, and quote the work order number. Phone numbers beat inbox searches here.
  3. A strata or body corporate. These pay on a meeting cycle. Ask when the next payment run is, rather than pressing for today, and confirm the invoice is addressed to the right entity.
  4. An after-hours callout nobody wrote up. The most common leak is not late payment but no invoice. Set the rule that the invoice goes out the next morning, whatever the hour of the callout.
  5. A dispute about the work. If a customer says the job was not done properly, stop the reminders. Our Invoice Chaser kit has a pause list so a disputed invoice does not keep generating automatic emails.

How to set it up, in order

  1. Fix the invoice first. Send it on the day the job finishes, with a due date and how to pay on it. Check it meets the ATO tax invoice requirements if you are registered for GST.
  2. Record the due date for every invoice in one place. A late reminder cannot start until you can see which invoices are past due.
  3. Decide the three touch points: 7, 14 and 30 days overdue. Write who sends them and what you do if there is no reply.
  4. If you use Xero, the Invoice Chaser for Xero kit reads open invoices each weekday and sends each stage once, starting in dry run mode so you can read every email before anything is sent. If you do not use Xero, send the same wording by hand.
  5. Set up a pause list for disputes and payment plans before you switch it on.
  6. Review monthly: how many invoices paid after reminder one, two and three? That tells you which message is doing the work.

Spam Act 2003 and reminders to a customer you have billed

An invoice reminder to a customer who owes you money is a message about an existing transaction, not marketing. The Spam Act 2003 applies to commercial electronic messages, and the ACMA's guidance says a message must identify the sender and, if it is marketing, carry an unsubscribe option honoured within 5 working days.

Keep reminders factual and keep promotions out of them. Do not add "book your next service" to a reminder, because that turns it into a message that promotes your business. Include your business name and contact details in every email and, for an SMS, a clear opt-out such as Reply STOP.

Invoices themselves have their own rules. The ATO sets what a tax invoice must show, including the words tax invoice, your ABN, the date, a description and the GST amount, with extra buyer details at $1,000 or more. This is general information, not legal advice.

Primary sources: ACMA: Avoid sending spam (Spam Act 2003 rules); Spam Act 2003, legislation.gov.au; ATO: Tax invoices.

Questions plumbers ask

How soon should a plumber chase an unpaid invoice?

Send the first reminder around 7 days after the due date, the second at 14 days and the last at 30 days, then talk to the customer by phone. These timings are a suggested cadence, not a measured standard.

Can I charge interest on a late plumbing invoice?

Only if your written terms with the customer allow it. The kit's reminders add no interest, fees or threats. Get advice before any formal recovery step.

Do I need Xero to use these messages?

No. The wording works by hand in any email. The kit automates the sending for Xero users.

What should I do when a customer disputes the work?

Pause the reminders, ring the customer and work out what went wrong. Only resume once the dispute is settled.
