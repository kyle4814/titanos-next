Plumbers x missed calls

Missed call text back for plumbers: the wording, the setup and a worked example

A plumber's phone rings most when both hands are busy: under a vanity, in a roof, behind a hot water unit. The caller with water on the floor does not leave a voicemail. They ring the next name on Google. This page gives you the exact text to send back, what to ask in it, how to count what missed calls cost you, and where the Spam Act 2003 sits.

By Kyle Deligny, Titanos, Brisbane. Last reviewed 28 November 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Why this is different for plumbers

Plumbing enquiries are urgent more often than most trades. A blocked drain, a failed hot water system or a leaking tap is a problem the customer wants fixed today, so the first plumber to reply tends to get the conversation. That is the reason a short, fast reply matters more here than a polished one.

The reply also does a second job for you: it sorts the work. One question, such as whether it is a leak, a blocked drain or hot water, tells you in ten seconds which call to return first when you climb down from the job.

Five plumbing situations and what the text should do in each

  1. Water is leaking now. A flexible hose has let go under a vanity at 6.40am. The caller wants the first plumber who responds. The after-hours text tells them to turn off the main tap, which is the one thing they can do before you arrive, and asks what is happening so you can ring back with a plan.
  2. Blocked drain while you are on another job. You are under a house on a stormwater repair. The caller has a slow kitchen sink. This is a job you can usually book for later that day, so the text asks one thing: is water coming back up, or just draining slowly? Their one-line answer tells you whether to ring in ten minutes or at smoko.
  3. Hot water failure on a cold morning. The customer has no hot water and a house full of people. The type of system matters for parts and for your quote: electric storage, gas continuous flow or solar. A reply that names the unit lets you arrive with the right fittings.
  4. A property manager with a tenant's leak. Agents and strata managers ring on behalf of someone else and need a job reference and an approval limit. Your text can ask for the address and who will give access. Managers who get a fast, businesslike reply often send the next job to the same plumber.
  5. A smell of gas. Do not rely on an automatic text for this one. Decide in advance what your voicemail and your after-hours text say about gas and emergencies, and make sure the first line sends the caller to 000 or the gas distributor's emergency line. The kit's templates cover the water case; write your own gas wording before you switch anything on.

The exact wording: in hours, after hours and the reply

The first two messages are the plumber templates from our Missed-Call Text-Back Kit, word for word. The others are extra wording written for this page. Replace the bracketed parts and keep each text to one question.

Property manager versionUse when the number is saved as an agency
Written for this page
Hi, it's [business]. Sorry we missed your call. Please reply with the property address, who gives access and your job reference, and we'll call [when].
  • Fill [when] with a real promise you keep, such as "within 30 minutes" in hours or "at 7.30am" after hours. A specific time beats "soon".
  • Do not quote a price in the first text. You have not seen the job, and a number sent blind is hard to take back.
  • Add "Reply STOP to opt out" to the end if your system does not do it for you. That is the wording ACMA itself gives as a clear SMS example.

How to set it up, in order

  1. Count first. Over two weeks, note every incoming call and how many went unanswered. Your phone's call history or your job app's call log has it. Without this count you cannot tell whether the fix is worth doing.
  2. Check what you already own. Some phone providers and job-management apps can send a missed call text. Look there before you build anything.
  3. If you want to run it yourself, the Missed-Call Text-Back Kit has two importable n8n workflows, one for Twilio and one for Cellcast or any phone system that can call a webhook. It skips landlines and withheld numbers and avoids texting the same caller twice.
  4. Edit the settings: business name, your mobile for the alert, opening hours and the [when] wording. The kit refuses to send until you have replaced the placeholders.
  5. Test it. Ring the number from a spare phone, let it ring out, and read the text that arrives. Do it once in hours and once after hours.
  6. Check the log after a week. Count the replies, count the jobs booked from them, and compare with your example numbers.

Spam Act 2003: is it allowed to text someone who rang you?

The Spam Act 2003 covers commercial electronic messages, and an SMS is one. The ACMA, which enforces the Act, says that if you send marketing messages you need the recipient's consent, the message must accurately identify you as the sender, and it must carry a working way to unsubscribe that you honour within 5 working days.

A single text that answers the call the person just made to you, about the job they rang about, is a different thing from marketing sent to a list. Whether a given message counts as commercial depends on what it says, so keep the reply to their enquiry, name your business, add the opt-out line and do not use the same system to send offers. If you later want to send promotions, you need consent for those, and the ACMA notes you cannot send a message to ask for consent, because that message is itself marketing.

A caller's phone number can be personal information. If your business is covered by the Privacy Act 1988, handle it under the Australian Privacy Principles, and tell people in your privacy policy that you may text those who ring you. Many small businesses under AU$3 million turnover are exempt, with exceptions, so check with the OAIC. This is general information, not legal advice.

Primary sources: ACMA: Avoid sending spam (Spam Act 2003 rules); Spam Act 2003, legislation.gov.au; OAIC: Australian Privacy Principles.

Questions plumbers ask

Is missed call text back legal for plumbers in Australia?

A reply to a person who has just rung you about a job, limited to that enquiry and carrying your business name and an opt-out line, is the low-risk end of the Spam Act. Anything that promotes your services to people who did not ask is marketing and needs consent. Read the ACMA guidance and get advice if unsure.

What should a plumber say in a missed call text?

Say who you are, that you missed the call because you are on a job, and ask one question that sorts the work, such as leak, blocked drain or hot water. Promise a call back at a stated time.

Does the kit use AI or record calls?

No. It is a plain rules-based n8n workflow with no AI and no call recording. It texts the caller and alerts you.

What about callers on a landline?

A landline cannot receive a text, so the kit skips landlines and withheld numbers. Keep a voicemail greeting for those callers.
