AusTender Contract Awards: Find Subbie Work

← Back to the archive
logged 28 November 2026 · 9 min read

Most trade businesses never look at AusTender, because the tenders on it are usually too big, too formal or too far away. That is a fair instinct for the open tenders. The part almost nobody reads is the record of contracts that have already been awarded. Every one of those names a company that has just won a job and may need local subcontractors to deliver it. This guide shows how to read that record for free, what it can and cannot tell you, and how to approach a head contractor without being a nuisance.

A clear caution first. An awarded contract is not an open tender. You cannot bid for it. It is information about who holds the work, not an invitation. We describe it as intelligence for sales conversations, not as a list of jobs you can win.

What AusTender is

AusTender is the Australian Government's central site for publishing procurement. It shows open approaches to market, annual procurement plans and, most usefully here, contract notices. Under the Commonwealth Procurement Rules, government entities report the contracts they enter above a set value, and the notice lists the supplier, the buying agency, the value, the dates and a description of what was bought. Reporting thresholds and timing depend on the type of entity and the rules in force, so check the current Commonwealth Procurement Rules if the exact detail matters.

Two limits to remember. AusTender covers Commonwealth government purchasing, not state, local council or private work, which have their own sites. And a contract notice shows the contract value, not what the head contractor pays subcontractors.

Why awarded contracts are useful to a subcontractor

  • They name who won. A building services or facilities company with a new multi-year contract needs capacity.
  • They show where the work is. A description or agency can point to a base, a site or a region.
  • They show how long it runs. A contract with a period of several years suggests ongoing demand.
  • They are public. You are using published records, not guessing at a private database.

Think of a Queensland tradie who does air conditioning maintenance. If a facilities management company wins a three-year contract to maintain a federal building in Brisbane, that company may need a local air conditioning subcontractor. A conversation with them could make more sense than watching the tender portal for a bid you could not realistically win.

Step by step: finding awards near you

  1. Go to AusTender at tenders.gov.au and open the contract notices search. You do not need an account to search published notices.
  2. Filter by what you do. AusTender uses UNSPSC category codes. Code 72, building and facility construction and maintenance services, is the family most relevant to trades. Use the description field for words such as plumbing, electrical, air conditioning or cleaning.
  3. Filter by date. Start with the last 90 days. Recent awards are the ones where the winner is still lining up delivery partners.
  4. Look at the agency and description for a location. Not every notice names a site, so some detective work is needed.
  5. Make a short list of winners with a plausible link to your trade and area. Note the supplier name, the value and the contract period.
  6. Research each supplier. Look at their website, whether they list subcontractor or supplier registration, and who handles procurement.

A faster way for the technically inclined

AusTender also offers a free, key-free data service that follows the Open Contracting Data Standard (OCDS). You can request contract notices published between two dates and receive structured JSON. We checked the service on 28 November 2026 using a one-day window of early November 2026 and it returned 24 releases. Each record included the supplier name and ABN, the buying agency, the contract value in Australian dollars, the start and end dates, a description and a UNSPSC code.

https://api.tenders.gov.au/ocds/findByDates/contractPublished/2026-11-01T00:00:00Z/2026-11-02T00:00:00Z

The data is published under a Creative Commons Attribution licence. Anyone comfortable with a spreadsheet or a short script can filter by UNSPSC code, region keywords and value, and produce a short list in minutes. If you are not technical, the website search above does the same job more slowly.

Reading a contract notice

Value

The amount is the contract value, which may be spread over several years and may include options. It does not tell you what is available to subcontractors.

Period

A multi-year period means repeat work. A short one means a project that may finish before you can get involved.

Description

Descriptions vary from precise to cryptic. Look for words that match what you do, and for a place name.

Procurement method

Open tender, select tender or limited tender tell you how competitive the process was. A panel arrangement means the winner is one of several on a list.

Approaching a head contractor without being annoying

The winner has probably been contacted by a hundred salespeople. Make yours useful and specific.

  • Say exactly what you do and where. A line such as we do commercial air conditioning servicing across Brisbane and Moreton Bay is far more useful than general capability.
  • Make joining easy. Attach or link your licence number, insurance certificates, safety documentation and references, because head contractors need them before they can engage anyone.
  • Ask a question, not for work. Do you have a supplier registration process for local subcontractors? is easy to answer.
  • Use a phone call or a form on their website for the first contact. If you do email, keep it relevant to the person's role.
  • Be patient. A head contractor is more likely to use you when a need arises than on the day you write.

Cold emails to business addresses are still covered by the Spam Act 2003. Messages must be relevant to the recipient's role, identify you accurately and offer a way to opt out. Our guide to the Spam Act explains how those rules apply.

Other places to look

AusTender is for Commonwealth work. State and territory governments publish their own awards and opportunities, such as QTenders in Queensland, NSW eTendering and Tenders VIC. Local councils often publish on their own websites. The same approach works: find the winners, then approach them as a local subcontractor.

What not to do

  • Do not claim a connection you do not have, or imply that an agency endorses you.
  • Do not present an awarded contract as an open opportunity to anyone, including yourself.
  • Do not scrape personal contact details beyond what is published for business use.
  • Do not spend a month on this before checking whether your insurances and licences meet typical head-contractor requirements.

How this fits with the rest of your pipeline

Contract awards are one source of conversations. Customers who already know you are still the cheapest work to win, so make sure you are answering calls and following up quotes before chasing larger sources.
