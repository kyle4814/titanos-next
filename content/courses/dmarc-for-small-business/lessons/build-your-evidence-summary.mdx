## Prove it to a client or insurer

Sooner or later a supplier questionnaire, a tender or an insurer will ask something like "Do you protect your domain against spoofing?" A clear one-page answer makes that conversation short and calm.

### What a good summary contains

Keep it to a single page that a non-technical person can read. Use only facts you have checked yourself.

1. **Domain and date.** The domain covered and the date you last checked.
2. **SPF.** Present, single record, and what it ends with.
3. **DKIM.** Which of your sending services sign with your domain.
4. **DMARC.** The policy (none, quarantine or reject), whether a reporting address is set, and the subdomain policy.
5. **Non-sending domains.** The names you own that never send mail, and that they are locked down.
6. **Monitoring.** How often you check, and who is responsible.
7. **Changes.** Dates you moved from monitor to quarantine to reject.

### Evidence worth keeping

Save these in one folder, with dates:

- A screenshot or copy of each lookup result.
- The text of your records before and after each change.
- A report summary showing your known senders passing.
- The register and tracker from the worksheets.
- A copy of your free scan result, if you ran one.

Evidence that shows a record on a certain date is much stronger than a statement of what you meant to do.

### Be honest about limits

If you are still at p=none, say so, with the date you plan to move. A truthful "monitoring, enforcement planned by this date" is far better than a claim you cannot support. Reviewers generally prefer a clear plan to an overclaim. Do not state any score, certification or percentage you have not measured. The summary supports your answer, but it is not an audit or a certificate.

### Your steps

1. Open the evidence summary worksheet and fill in each line from your records.
2. Ask your IT provider to read it for accuracy before you send it out.
3. Save it as a PDF with the date in the file name.
4. Put a diary note to refresh it each quarter, or after any major change.
