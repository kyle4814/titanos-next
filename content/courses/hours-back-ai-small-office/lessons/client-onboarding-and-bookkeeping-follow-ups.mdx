## Starting well, and keeping the books moving

New clients need the same things from you each time: a welcome, a list of what you need from them, and a clear idea of what happens next. Bookkeeping needs a steady stream of nudges for receipts, statements and questions. Both involve a lot of chasing for small pieces of information.

## Client onboarding

Write down your actual onboarding steps first, in order. Most offices find that the steps exist only in someone's head. A drafting tool cannot invent your process, so it helps to be clear about it.

> "I run a small Australian office. Here are our onboarding steps: [list]. Write a warm welcome email for a new client that explains these steps in plain language, lists what we need from them, and says who their contact is. Leave [square brackets] for anything specific to the client."

Build a checklist to go with it.

1. Welcome message approved and sent.
2. Forms and documents requested.
3. Information received and checked by a person.
4. First meeting or call booked.
5. Notes filed in your usual system.

If you collect identity documents, tax file numbers or bank details, that is sensitive material. Do not paste it into an AI tool. Handle it through the secure method your office already uses.

## Bookkeeping follow-ups

If you are a bookkeeper or you chase for one, the repetitive part is asking clients for missing paperwork. The same request, with small changes, goes out again and again.

> "Draft a friendly email to a client asking for the bank statements for [month] and any receipts for purchases over [your threshold]. Explain that we need them by [date] so their records are up to date. Keep it under 100 words, no jargon."

> "Write a second reminder, a little firmer, that thanks them if they have already sent them."

Track which clients are outstanding on a simple sheet. The tool drafts, you check the dates and names, and a person approves.

## Questions about accounts

Do not rely on a tool to say what is deductible, what a rule is, or how to treat a transaction. Tax and accounting treatment depends on the facts. Ask your registered tax agent or accountant. A tool can help you phrase the question, not answer it for you.

> "Help me write a clear, short question to my accountant about how to handle [describe the situation in general terms, no names or numbers]."

## Keep the human touch

Onboarding is the first impression your office makes. Use drafts as a starting point and add a line that only you would write, like the name of the person who referred them. People notice the difference between a template and a welcome.

## Measure it

Log how long onboarding and document chasing take for the next three clients. Include the time you spent reading and fixing drafts. Compare it with your week one figure.

- [ ] I wrote our real onboarding steps down first.
- [ ] No sensitive identity or bank details went into a tool.
- [ ] A person approved every message.
