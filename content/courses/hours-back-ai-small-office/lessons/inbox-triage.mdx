## The inbox that never finishes

For many small offices, the inbox is the biggest single drain. It is not just the replying. It is the reading, the deciding, the flagging, and the going back to find the one thing someone asked on Tuesday. Triage means sorting by urgency before you act, and AI is good at sorting text.

## What triage looks like

You are not asking the tool to answer your email. You are asking it to help you decide what to look at first. Sort each message into four piles.

1. Needs a reply today.
2. Needs a reply this week.
3. For information only, no reply needed.
4. Belongs to someone else, so pass it on.

## A safe way to do it

Check your email provider's settings first, because some already offer built-in sorting. Then use a plain approach that needs no special access: copy only the text of a few messages, with names, phone numbers, account numbers and anything private removed, and paste them into your assistant.

> "Below are five emails with personal details removed. For each one, tell me in one line what is being asked, whether it is urgent, and what a sensible next step is. Do not write replies yet."

Read its sorting against the originals. You will quickly learn where it misjudges tone or urgency. A message that says "no rush" from a long-time client might actually be urgent in context, and only you know that.

## Drafting replies for the routine ones

Many replies are the same few answers: opening hours, how to send a document, when a job will start, what happens next. Build a small library of approved answers once, then use it.

> "Draft a short, friendly reply to a client who asked when we are next available. Our usual lead time is [your own figure]. Do not promise a date. Say we will confirm after checking the diary. Sign off as [name]."

You supply the facts in square brackets. The tool supplies the wording. You check both.

## What not to automate

Do not let any tool reply to complaints, upset clients, disputes or anything where tone is the whole message. Do not let it decide who matters. Do not forward your whole inbox into an outside tool. If that is ever on the table, ask an IT professional first, and read the tool's privacy terms.

## Make it a short, fixed slot

The real saving comes from batching. Pick two or three set times a day for the inbox. At each, run the triage prompt on that batch, handle pile one, and schedule pile two. Your log from lesson one will show how many minutes the inbox took. Keep logging for a week afterwards so you can compare.

- [ ] I removed personal details before pasting anything.
- [ ] I checked the sorting against the real messages.
- [ ] I have two or three fixed inbox times.
