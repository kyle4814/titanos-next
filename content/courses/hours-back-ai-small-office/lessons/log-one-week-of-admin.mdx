## The evenings you did not plan to work

If you run a small office, you probably know the feeling. The day's real work is done, and then the admin arrives: the inbox, the quotes nobody answered, the invoices still unpaid, the notes from a meeting you cannot quite remember. It follows you home.

This course is about handing some of those hours back to you. Before we start, three promises that shape everything that follows.

- Nobody is replaced. The aim is to give people their hours back, not to take their jobs.
- You approve everything. AI drafts, a human reads it, and a human decides whether it goes out.
- You need nothing new. The lessons work with the email, spreadsheet and AI assistant you can already use. Nothing here needs TITANOS to see your data or your accounts.

## Why we measure first

The job that feels worst is not always the job that takes longest. So before you change anything, you count. This is the same rule TITANOS applies to its own work: measure first, claim second.

## How to log one week

You do not need software. A notepad or a spreadsheet will do. For five working days, each time you finish an admin task, write down three things.

1. What the task was, in a few plain words.
2. Roughly how many minutes it took.
3. Whether it was a repeat job (you did it last week and will again next week).

Ask everyone who handles admin to log their own time, and tell them why. This is a measuring exercise, not a performance check.

Round to the nearest five minutes. Rough and honest beats exact and abandoned.

### Group the entries

At the end of the week, group the lines under headings such as inbox, quotes, invoices and payments, meeting notes, rosters and reminders, onboarding new clients, and bookkeeping follow-ups. Add up the minutes in each group. Then mark every line that was a repeat job.

Repeat jobs are your candidates. A job you do the same way every time, with words you keep retyping, is the best kind of job to hand to a drafting tool.

## A prompt to help you sort

If your log is messy, you can ask an AI assistant to tidy it. Only paste the task descriptions and minutes, never names or client details.

> "Here is a list of admin tasks with minutes. Group them into sensible headings, add up the minutes for each heading, and list the three headings that take the most time. Do not guess anything I have not told you."

Check its sums with a calculator. AI tools can slip on arithmetic, which is a good early reminder that you always check.
