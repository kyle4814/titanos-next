## Your numbers, not ours

It is easy to be told that a tool saves time. It is harder, and more useful, to find out whether it saved yours. This lesson shows you how to measure your own before and after, with no help from anyone's marketing.

TITANOS follows a simple rule on its own work: measure first, claim second. Each figure it publishes carries a method, a date and a label saying whether it was measured or modelled. You can use the same discipline in a small office with a pen and a spreadsheet.

## What to compare

You already have the before: your one-week log from lesson one, and the figure for the job you chose. Now you need an after that is fair.

1. Use the same job and the same measuring method.
2. Run the new way for at least a week, or for a set number of cases, such as ten follow-ups.
3. Count all the time: writing the instruction, waiting, reading, correcting, approving and sending. A saving that ignores checking is not a saving.
4. Log the same people, in the same way.

## The sums

For one job, the figures you need are simple.

- Minutes per case before.
- Minutes per case after, including checking.
- Number of cases in a typical week.

Minutes saved per case multiplied by cases per week gives you minutes back per week. Divide by sixty for hours. Write both numbers down, with the date and how you measured.

If the answer is small, or negative, that is a result too. It may mean the job was not a good one to hand over, or the instruction needs work. Do not hide it. Change one thing, such as the prompt wording, and measure again.

## What counts and what does not

Count time that came back to a person and was used on something else, or let them leave on time. Do not count time that was only moved elsewhere, such as five minutes saved drafting and five minutes lost fixing mistakes. Do not count guesses about what you might have done.

Also watch quality. If replies are faster but clients are confused, or mistakes are slipping through, record that as well. Hours back should not cost you your reputation.

## Ask the people doing the work

Numbers tell part of the story. Ask the team: Which part felt lighter? What still takes too long? Did anyone leave on time? Write down what they say in their own words.

> "Here are my before and after minutes for one job. Check my arithmetic and tell me the minutes saved per week. Do not add any figures I have not given you."

Then check it yourself with a calculator.

## Fill in the results sheet

Use the before and after results sheet worksheet. Keep the finished sheet. It is your evidence, for yourself and your team, and it tells you what to hand over next.

- [ ] I measured the same job the same way.
- [ ] I counted the checking time.
- [ ] I wrote down what the team said.
