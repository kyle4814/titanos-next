## Notes, rosters and reminders

Three more jobs that quietly eat time in a small office: writing up what was decided in a meeting, building or adjusting the roster, and sending the reminders that stop things being missed. All three are mostly words and patterns, which makes them good candidates.

## Meeting notes

Most meeting notes are really a list of who agreed to do what by when. A drafting tool can turn your rough jottings into that list. Use your own typed notes, not a recording. Check your team is comfortable first, and before using any recording or transcription tool, ask a professional about consent rules where you are.

> "Here are my rough notes from a team meeting, with client names removed. Turn them into three headings: decisions made, actions with a person and a date, and open questions. Only use what is in my notes. If a person or date is missing, write [missing]."

The "only use what is in my notes" line matters. Without it, tools sometimes fill gaps with plausible guesses. Read the result with the meeting in your head, and send it to attendees only after you have approved it.

## Rosters

A roster is a puzzle with human rules: availability, leave, fairness, who cannot work together, who must hold a licence for certain tasks. A tool can suggest a first layout, but you are responsible for the final one, including award and agreement obligations, which depend on your situation. Ask your adviser if unsure.

> "Here are the availability notes for four staff for next week, with first names only. Suggest a roster where each person gets between [your minimum] and [your maximum] shifts, and flag any gap in coverage. Do not assume anyone is available unless I have said so."

Never share a staff member's health, family or personal circumstances with a tool. Say "unavailable Tuesday", not why.

## Reminders

Appointment reminders, "documents still needed" nudges and end-of-month prompts are a perfect pattern job. Write one good template with square-bracket gaps and reuse it.

> "Write a friendly reminder text for an appointment on [day] at [time] at [place]. Ask them to reply to confirm or call us to change it. Under forty words. Plain, warm, no exclamation marks."

Many diary and booking tools already send reminders. Check what you have before adding anything.

## Keep a prompt book

When a prompt works, save it. A single shared document called "Our prompts" with the job name, the wording, and the date you last checked it will save the next person from starting from scratch. It also helps when someone is away.

## Check the claim

Pick one of the three jobs and keep logging for a week. Your original log gave you a figure for minutes spent. Compare it with the new figure, and note what you spent on checking. Checking time counts, because it is real time.

- [ ] I only pasted notes with names and private details removed.
- [ ] I read every output against the real meeting or roster.
- [ ] I saved the working prompts in one shared place.
