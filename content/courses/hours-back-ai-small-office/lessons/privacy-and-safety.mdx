## Know what never goes in

AI tools are useful, and they are also outside services. Anything you paste into one has left your office. So decide, before you start, what never goes in. This is the single most useful safety habit in the whole course.

## Never paste these

- Passwords, security codes, recovery keys or anything that opens an account.
- Bank account numbers, card numbers and payment details.
- Tax file numbers, driver licence numbers, passport details and other identity documents.
- Health information about anyone.
- Staff files, performance matters, pay details and disciplinary notes.
- Full client lists, or anything under a confidentiality agreement.
- Legal advice you have received, or papers from a dispute.

When in doubt, leave it out. Replace names with "Client A" and amounts with square brackets. You can put the real details back after the draft arrives.

## The Privacy Act at a plain level

In Australia, the Privacy Act and the Australian Privacy Principles set rules for how personal information is collected, used, stored and disclosed. Some small businesses are covered and some are not, and some are covered because of what they do, such as handling health information. We cannot tell you which applies to you, and this course is not legal advice. Ask your lawyer, accountant or IT professional.

In plain terms, the ideas behind the rules are sensible for everyone.

1. Collect only what you need.
2. Tell people what you do with their information.
3. Use it for the purpose they would expect.
4. Keep it secure, and delete it when you no longer need it.
5. If something goes wrong with personal information, find out quickly what your obligations are.

Sending a client's personal details to an outside tool is a disclosure to think about. Check the tool's terms, including where data is stored and whether it is used to train the tool.

## Keep your own data and keys

A principle TITANOS holds to: clients keep their own data and their own keys. Your accounts and logins stay with you. Nothing in this course asks you to give anyone access to your systems, and you should be wary of any tool or provider that asks for more access than the job needs. If you use an IT provider, keep them in the loop, since they stay in charge of your systems.

## Safer habits

- Use a business account for your AI tool, not a personal one, with a strong unique password and two-step sign-in.
- Check what the tool does with your inputs, and switch off history or training use where the settings allow.
- Write your never-paste list on the approval rule page.
- Treat any unexpected message asking you to change bank details as a possible scam, whoever wrote it, and phone to confirm using a number you already have.
