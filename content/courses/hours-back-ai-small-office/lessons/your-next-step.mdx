## What you have now

By this point you have done something most people only talk about. You have counted where your hours go, chosen one job, written an approval rule, tried drafting for real work, thought about what must never be pasted into a tool, and measured a result with your own numbers.

Whatever the figures showed, you now know more about your office than you did a few weeks ago. That is worth having even if you decide a particular job should stay fully human.

## Keep the rhythm going

A good habit is a short monthly review. Put it in the diary for an hour at the same time each month and ask four questions.

1. Which jobs did we hand over, and what did they save, by our own measure?
2. What did the approver catch? Keep a list of mistakes found in drafts. It is not a failure, it is proof the rule works.
3. What does the team want to hand over next?
4. Has anything changed in the tools or their privacy terms?

Then choose the next job and repeat: log, score, trial, approve, measure.

## A few reminders worth keeping

- AI drafts, and a human approves every message.
- Nobody is replaced. The goal is hours given back.
- Measure before and after with your own numbers.
- Your data and your keys stay with you.
- People stay in charge.

When a tool makes a mistake, treat it like a new staff member's first draft: correct it, learn from it, and give clearer instructions next time. If something feels wrong, stop and ask a professional.

## A prompt for the road

> "Here is a job we do every week: [describe it without private details]. List what a person must always decide or check, and what could safely be drafted for review."

Use the answer as a starting point only, and compare it with your approval rule.
