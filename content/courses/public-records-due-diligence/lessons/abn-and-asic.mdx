## The two registers that settle the basics

If you only ever do two checks, make them these. They answer the plainest questions: is this entity real, and is it what it says it is?

## ABN Lookup

ABN Lookup at abr.business.gov.au is the public view of the Australian Business Register. Search by ABN or by name. What you can usually see includes the entity name, entity type, whether the ABN is active or cancelled, the date it took effect, GST registration status, and any business names recorded. Always read the registry's own page to confirm exactly what it currently shows.

What to compare against what you were told:

- Does the legal name match the name on the quote, invoice or contract?
- Is the ABN active, and since when?
- Is the entity type what you expected: company, trust, partnership or sole trader?
- Is the GST status consistent with what appears on their invoices?

A cancelled ABN, or a name that does not match, is not proof of anything bad. It is a question to put to the other party before you sign. Plenty of honest businesses have an out of date invoice template.

## ASIC registers

If the counterparty is a company, the ASIC registers at asic.gov.au add more. Depending on the search, you can find the company status (registered, deregistered, in liquidation and so on), the registration date, the type of company, and officeholders such as directors. Some extracts are free and some are paid, so check ASIC's own page before ordering anything.

ASIC also publishes insolvency notices, such as the appointment of a liquidator or administrator. These are published for exactly the purpose of letting creditors and others know. If you are about to extend credit or pay a deposit, search for the company name here.

## Reading the dates

Dates matter more than most readers expect. A company registered very recently asking for large credit terms is worth a question, though a new company can be perfectly sound. A long history with a recent change of directors is likewise a prompt to ask, not a conclusion.

Record the date you ran each search. Registers change, and your note should show what was true on the day you looked.

## Sole traders and trusts

A sole trader has no ASIC company record, so the ABN record carries most of the weight. A trust is often represented by a trustee company, so the trustee is the entity to check. If you cannot work out who the contracting entity is, that itself is a finding, and the answer is to ask them.

## Do this now

Use your chosen organisation.

1. Search its ABN on ABN Lookup and write down the name, entity type, status and effective date.
2. Compare each against your last invoice or contract. Note any mismatch.
3. If it is a company, search ASIC for its status and any insolvency notice.
4. Put the date you searched next to every line.
5. Enter each result in the finding log worksheet, marked observed.
