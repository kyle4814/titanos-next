## Three more public views of an organisation

These sources do not apply to every counterparty, but when they do, they add a useful angle: who else trusts them, whether they are legally allowed to be dealt with, and what has been said publicly.

## AusTender

AusTender at tenders.gov.au publishes Australian Government contract notices, including awarded contracts. If your counterparty claims to supply government, search for their name or ABN in the awarded contracts. You can see which agency awarded the contract, the dates, the category and the stated value.

Use it to test claims. If a supplier says "we work with several federal agencies" and the record shows a few awarded contracts, that is a consistent picture. If it shows nothing, note it, but remember that subcontractors and state or local government work often do not appear. Absence is an open question, not a finding against them.

## The DFAT consolidated list

The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade publishes a consolidated list of persons and entities subject to Australian sanctions and similar listings. Dealing with a listed party can be a serious legal matter. For most small teams, searching the list takes a few minutes and is worth doing for any new overseas supplier, buyer or intermediary, or where money will move across borders. Check the DFAT page for how the list is published and updated.

If you get a possible match, do not decide for yourself. Names can be similar. Stop, record exactly what you saw, and get advice from a lawyer before going further. This is one of the clearest stop points in the course.

## News and public statements

A news search on the organisation's name and its directors can reveal things registers do not, such as a regulator action, a product recall, a data breach announcement or a dispute. Handle news with extra care.

- Prefer the original source, such as a regulator's media release, over a summary.
- Note the publication date. Old stories may have been resolved.
- Check that the story is actually about this entity and not one with a similar name.
- Treat opinion and allegation as exactly that.

## The organisation's own filings

Annual reports, published policies, and statements on their website are also public records. They are claims, and a good check sets claims beside what the registers show.

## Do this now

For your chosen organisation:

1. If it sells to government, search AusTender by name or ABN. Note the result either way.
2. If it is overseas, or money crosses borders, search the DFAT consolidated list for the name and key people.
3. Run a news search on the entity name. Record the three most relevant items with source and publication date.
4. Mark each result observed, and write any reading of it as inferred.
5. Add them to your finding log.
