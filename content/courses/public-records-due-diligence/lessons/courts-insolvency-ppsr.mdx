## What courts, AFSA and the PPSR can tell you

The basic registers tell you an entity exists. This lesson looks at what has gone wrong for it in public: disputes, insolvency and debts secured against assets. Read it with care, because this is where careless conclusions do the most damage.

## Court and tribunal judgments

Courts and tribunals across the states, territories and the Commonwealth publish many of their judgments online, and each has its own search tool. Search by the exact entity name and, where relevant, the names of its directors. Each site explains what it covers, so read the page before relying on a blank result.

Three cautions apply.

- **A blank search is not a clean bill.** Some matters are not published, and some are settled privately.
- **Name matches are not identity matches.** Common names appear in many unrelated cases. Match on more than a name, such as location or the ABN where it is quoted.
- **A case is not a verdict.** A claim filed against an organisation is an allegation until decided. Record what the document says, and who the parties are.

## AFSA: bankruptcy

The Australian Financial Security Authority (afsa.gov.au) runs the National Personal Insolvency Index, which records bankruptcies and related arrangements for individuals. It matters most for sole traders and partnerships, where the person and the business are the same legal entity. Check AFSA's page for how to search and what is shown.

## The PPSR

The Personal Property Securities Register is also run through AFSA. It shows security interests registered against assets, such as equipment or vehicles. For a supplier, a registration may show that a lender or another supplier has a claim over certain goods. That is common in ordinary business and often means nothing alarming. It can matter if you are buying a second hand item or if goods you are paying for might be subject to someone else's claim.

A PPSR search carries a small fee set by government. Check the PPSR's own page for the current arrangements, and decide whether the exposure justifies the search.

## Facts, not verdicts

When you write any of this up, use the form: "The [register] showed [what it showed] on [date]." Do not write "they are in trouble" or "they are dodgy". That is a conclusion you cannot support from a registry, and it is how people end up defaming others, which the ethics lesson covers.

## Do this now

For your chosen organisation:

1. Search the relevant court and tribunal sites for the exact entity name. Note which sites you searched, even where nothing came up.
2. If it is a sole trader or partnership, check AFSA for personal insolvency.
3. Decide whether a PPSR search is justified by your exposure. Write your reason either way.
4. Log every result, including blank ones, with date and source.
5. Mark each as observed. Put any guess about meaning in a separate inferred line.
