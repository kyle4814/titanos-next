## Doing this fairly

Public record research is legitimate and ordinary. It still involves real people and real reputations, so a few firm habits keep you safe and fair.

## Public records only

The method works only with sources anyone can access. You do not log in to systems that are not yours, scan servers, open files you were not given, or ask staff to reveal things. You do not impersonate a customer, a regulator or a journalist to get information. If a step would require pretending or intruding, it is outside this method.

## Privacy Act awareness

Your findings will include names of directors and sometimes personal details. The Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles may apply to how organisations collect, hold and use personal information, depending on your size and circumstances. Some small businesses are exempt, and others are not. Check the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner website, or ask an adviser, if you are unsure.

Sensible habits regardless:

- Collect only what you need for the decision in your scope.
- Do not copy personal details beyond what the pre-read needs.
- Keep records securely and delete them when you no longer need them.
- Do not use what you find for anything other than the stated decision.

## Never defame

Defamation is publishing something that harms a person's or organisation's reputation without a defence. Your safest practice is the one you have used throughout. State facts with their source and date, keep inferences labelled as questions, and avoid conclusions about character or honesty. Keep the pre-read inside the decision group. Do not post findings on social media or share them in industry chat groups.

## When to stop and call a professional

You are a careful reader of public records, not a lawyer or accountant. Stop and get advice when:

- **A possible sanctions match** appears.
- **A court matter or insolvency notice** affects money you are owed or about to pay.
- **You suspect fraud or identity misuse.** Do not confront anyone. Take advice, and consider reporting through the proper channels.
- **The contract is large or hard to exit,** and the pre-read includes any red flag.
- **You are unsure how to read a legal document** you found.
- **You have a Privacy Act question** you cannot resolve from the regulator's guidance.

Bring your finding log and pre-read. It will make the conversation shorter and cheaper.

## Do this now

1. Write on your chosen organisation's pre-read where the records are stored and when you will delete them.
2. Check each line for facts with a source, not conclusions.
3. List which of the stop conditions above apply, if any, and who you would call.
4. Set a reminder to repeat the quick checks just before you sign.
