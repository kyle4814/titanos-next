## From notes to a decision

A finding log is for you. A pre-read is for the person who must decide, which might be you, a business partner or a board. It is one page. If a decision maker needs more than a page, the pre-read has not done its job.

## The shape of the page

Use the same sections each time so readers learn where to look.

1. **The question.** Who we checked and what decision it supports, copied from your scope.
2. **What we checked.** The sources searched and the dates, in a single line.
3. **What we found.** Three to six observed facts, each with its source. Lead with the ones that bear on the decision.
4. **Questions for the other party.** Inferences and flags turned into plain questions.
5. **What we could not check.** Honest gaps, such as no access to private financials or unpublished matters.
6. **Suggested next step.** Proceed, proceed with conditions, pause pending answers, or take advice first.

## Conditions that protect you

Often the right answer is neither yes nor no, but yes with conditions. A pre-read might suggest a smaller first order, payment on delivery, a confirmation of bank details by phone, or a short review by your accountant before extending credit. These are practical steps within your control.

## Wording that holds up

Write in short, factual sentences with the source in brackets.

- "ABN active since [date] (ABN Lookup, [date searched])."
- "No court judgments found under the entity name on the searched sites ([date])."
- "Company deregistered per ASIC register on [date]. Question: which entity will contract with us?"

Avoid adjectives about character. Avoid the words "fraud", "scam" or "liar" entirely. If you suspect something of that kind, you stop and take advice instead of writing it.

## Respect the limits of what you know

State the date on the page. A pre-read describes the public record on one day. If the signing is weeks away, repeat the quick checks, such as ABN status and insolvency notices, just before you sign.

## Who sees it

Share the pre-read only with people who need it for the decision. It names real people and organisations, so store it with your other business records and delete it when you no longer need it.

## Do this now

Open the pre-read template worksheet.

1. Fill in the six sections for your chosen organisation using your finding log.
2. Keep it to one page.
3. Read it as if the organisation would see it. Fix any line you could not defend by pointing to a source.
4. Decide your suggested next step and write it plainly.
