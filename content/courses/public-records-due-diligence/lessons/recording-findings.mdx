## If you did not write it down, you did not find it

Searching is the easy part. The value comes from a record that someone else, including future you, can pick up and trust. A good finding log has the same few fields every time.

## The five fields

1. **Finding.** What you saw, in one plain sentence.
2. **Source.** The register or page, named exactly, for example ABN Lookup or the ASIC insolvency notices.
3. **Date.** The day you looked. Registers change.
4. **Confidence.** Observed or inferred, explained below.
5. **Next step.** Nothing, ask the counterparty, or get advice.

## Observed versus inferred

**Observed** means you saw it directly in a public source and can point to it. Example: "ABN Lookup showed the ABN as active on the day of the search."

**Inferred** means it is your reading, so it could be wrong. Example: "The trading name differs from the registered name, which may mean a recent rebrand."

Keep the two in separate lines. In the pre-read, observed facts carry the weight and inferences are labelled as questions to put to the other party.

## Record blanks too

"Searched the court sites for the entity name on this date, no results found" is a finding. It shows what you did, and it stops you or a colleague repeating the work. It does not mean the organisation is clean, and your wording should not say so.

## Keep a copy of the evidence

Where the source allows, save a copy of the page or take a dated screenshot for your own file. Keep it with the log, and store it as carefully as any business record, since it may include names of individuals.

## Write in neutral language

Compare these two lines.

- "The company is in trouble and the directors are hiding something."
- "The ASIC register showed the company status as deregistered on the date searched."

The second can be defended by pointing at the source. The first cannot, and it could cause real harm if shared. Write every log line so that it would still read fairly if the other organisation saw it.

## Link findings to the decision

Each finding should connect back to your scope. If it does not bear on your decision, note it and move on. The red flags lesson helps you sort what matters from what does not.

## Do this now

Open the finding log worksheet.

1. Copy in every result from your earlier searches on your chosen organisation.
2. Fill in all five fields for each one.
3. Rewrite any line that sounds like a conclusion into a plain statement of what the source showed.
4. Mark each observed or inferred.
5. Add one line for each blank search, stating the source and date.
