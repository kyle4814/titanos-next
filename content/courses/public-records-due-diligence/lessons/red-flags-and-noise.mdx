## Most findings are ordinary

After a few searches you will have a page of notes. Some of it matters and much of it does not. Sorting the two calmly is the skill that stops you either missing a problem or backing out of a fine deal.

## What tends to be a real red flag

These deserve a question before you commit, and some deserve advice.

- **The entity you would sign with cannot be found,** or the legal name and ABN do not match what you were given.
- **A cancelled ABN or a deregistered company** while they are still trading.
- **A published insolvency notice,** such as a liquidator or administrator appointment, particularly when you are about to pay a deposit or extend credit.
- **A possible sanctions list match.** This is a stop point.
- **Claims that registers contradict,** such as claiming government contracts that you cannot find, or an address that does not exist.
- **A request to change bank details by email** from a domain with weak email posture.
- **Repeated, similar court matters** concerning unpaid suppliers or employees, as recorded in the judgments themselves.

## What is usually noise

- **One old, resolved dispute,** common among organisations that have traded for a long time.
- **A PPSR registration from a lender,** because finance is routine.
- **A name match you cannot tie to this entity.**
- **A relaxed email policy alone** at a small business.
- **A new company** with no other warning sign.
- **Absence from AusTender** where government work was never claimed.

## Ask yourself three questions

1. Is it observed or inferred? Give inferences less weight.
2. Does it bear on my decision and my exposure from the scope?
3. Is there an ordinary explanation I can simply ask about?

## Combining signals

One flag rarely decides anything. Several that point the same direction, such as a new entity, a mismatched name and an urgent request for a deposit, together call for a slower process. Be especially cautious where urgency is used to rush you past your checks.

## Turning flags into questions

The best outcome of a flag is a polite question: "Your invoice shows a different company name from the registered one. Can you confirm which entity we are contracting with?" A reasonable counterparty will answer simply. How they respond is itself useful information, and it is gathered openly, with no tricks.

## Do this now

Using your finding log for the chosen organisation:

1. Sort each finding into red flag, noise or unclear.
2. Next to each red flag, write the question you would put to them.
3. Next to each noise item, write the ordinary explanation.
4. Mark any item that needs a lawyer or accountant. You will use this in the ethics lesson.
