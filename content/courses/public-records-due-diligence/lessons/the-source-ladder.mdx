## Start cheap, climb only as far as you need

Not every source is worth your time for every decision. A source ladder is simply an order of work. You start with the quickest, most reliable public sources and climb to slower ones only when the decision calls for it.

Here is the ladder this course uses.

1. **The organisation's own website and published documents.** Free, fast, and tells you what they claim about themselves.
2. **ABN Lookup (abr.business.gov.au).** Confirms the entity name, status and whether the ABN is active.
3. **ASIC registers (asic.gov.au).** For companies: status, officeholders, and any insolvency notices.
4. **Court and tribunal judgment search.** State and federal courts publish many judgments, and the search tools use general name terms.
5. **Australian Financial Security Authority (afsa.gov.au).** Personal insolvency and bankruptcy records, and the Personal Property Securities Register (PPSR).
6. **AusTender (tenders.gov.au).** Awarded government contracts, if the organisation sells to government.
7. **DNS and email posture.** Public settings that show how the domain is set up.
8. **News and sanctions lists.** Including the DFAT consolidated list.

Check each registry's own page for what it currently covers, how it searches, and any fee. A PPSR search carries a small government fee, so decide whether the decision justifies it before you run it.

## Why this order

The early rungs are free and fast, and they settle the basic question of whether the entity exists and is what it says it is. If an ABN is cancelled or the company name does not match, you may not need the later rungs at all. You have your answer and can ask the other party about it.

The later rungs cost more time, and some cost a small fee, so they make sense where the exposure is high.

## Observed versus inferred

Two ideas run through the whole ladder.

An **observed** finding is something you saw in a public source: the registry shows the status as active. An **inferred** finding is your reading of what it might mean: the company seems to have changed its directors, so perhaps there was a dispute. Observed facts are solid. Inferences are guesses until someone confirms them. You will record the difference every time.

## When to stop climbing

Stop when you have enough to make the decision at the depth you chose in your scope. More searching does not always mean more safety. It often means more noise, which the red flags lesson covers.
