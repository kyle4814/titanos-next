Can a worker slip between two of your samples?

Do this first. If a sampler reads the worker count on a timer, a worker that starts and finishes between two samples is recorded as zero, and every per-worker-hour number downstream becomes a lower bound rather than a measurement. No polling interval is safe for arbitrarily short workers; only per-worker start and end events are.

Definitions, so nobody argues later

Attempted
tasks dispatched
Accepted
an independent reviewer said ACCEPTED
Worker hours
summed active worker time, not wall clock
Attributable accepted
accepted results inside intervals where worker time was observed
Attribution coverage
attributable / accepted. Below 1, your cost per accepted is a lower bound, and the gap is not a correction factor

Limitations

  • Everything here is arithmetic on numbers you supply. It cannot tell you whether your acceptance standard is any good.
  • When attribution coverage is below 1 the true cost per accepted result is unknown. This tool shows you the bound and the conditional figure; it will not invent the number in between.
  • The comparison is a difference between two windows, not a controlled experiment. Confounders are yours to rule out.
  • No network, no analytics, no storage. Nothing you type leaves the page.
