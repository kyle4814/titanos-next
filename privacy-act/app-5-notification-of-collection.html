APP 5 notification of collection: the collection notice explained

APP 5 requires an organisation covered by the Privacy Act to take reasonable steps, at or before the time it collects personal information (or as soon as practicable afterwards), to tell the person the relevant matters in APP 5.2 or to make sure they are already aware of them. That is usually a short collection notice next to each form, plus the link to the full privacy policy.

By Kyle Deligny, Titanos, Brisbane. Last reviewed 28 November 2026 against the Privacy Act 1988 (Compilation No. 104, 4 June 2026).

General information, not legal advice. It describes the law as read on the review date for a small business owner. For a decision that matters, ask a privacy lawyer.

Who it applies to

  • Any APP entity that collects personal information, whether directly from the person or from someone else.
  • Solicited information (a form, a call, a booking) and unsolicited information you keep rather than destroy or de-identify.
  • A small business that falls outside the Act is not bound, but a covered one is, and so is any business that is a contractor to a covered customer through the contract.

Not sure whether the Act covers your business at all? Start with the small business exemption explainer or run the free checker.

What it requires

The standard: reasonable steps, at the right time

APP 5.1 asks for such steps, if any, as are reasonable in the circumstances to notify the person of the APP 5.2 matters that are reasonable, or to otherwise ensure they are aware of them. The timing is at or before collection or, if that is not practicable, as soon as practicable after. The OAIC's guidelines read 'reasonable steps' as depending on things like how sensitive the information is, who the person is and how it is collected. A notice is not needed for a matter the person already knows, but you should be able to show why you thought they knew.

The matters you may need to cover (APP 5.2)

The identity and contact details of your business. The fact and circumstances of collection where you collected from someone else or the person may not be aware you collected it. Whether an Australian law or a court or tribunal order requires or authorises the collection, naming the law. The purposes of collection. The main consequences for the person if some or all of the information is not collected. The other bodies or types of bodies you usually disclose that kind of information to. A statement that your privacy policy explains how to access and correct information. A statement that it explains how to complain. Whether you are likely to disclose the information to overseas recipients and, if so and practicable, the countries.

How APP 5 connects to the rest

The overseas items in APP 5.2(i) and (j) mirror APP 1.4(f) and (g), so a notice and a policy that disagree is a common fault. If you start using a new tool that sends data offshore (an AI transcription service, a new CRM) the notice needs updating, and APP 8 may need attention too. From 10 December 2026, if automated decisions fall inside APP 1.7, the policy the notice points to must say so.

Worked example: a 9-person landscaping firm's online quote form

A 9-person Gold Coast landscaping business collects name, mobile, email, street address and photos of the yard through a web quote form. It also takes the same details by phone. Turnover is AU$3.4 million, so the Act covers it. It uses a third-party CRM hosted offshore and a review-request tool.

  1. Identity and contact: business name, ABN and a privacy email address under the form.
  2. Purpose: to prepare and deliver the quote, schedule the work and send a review request after the job.
  3. Consequence of not providing: 'we cannot quote without the property address'. Optional fields are marked optional.
  4. Disclosure: the CRM provider, the scheduling tool and the review-request tool, described as types of provider if names change often.
  5. Policy pointers: 'Our privacy policy explains how to see and correct your information and how to complain', with the link.
  6. Overseas: 'Our CRM provider stores data in the United States'. This is the only country that is practicable to state, and it must match the privacy policy.
  7. Phone collection: the same matters are said in one sentence at the start of the call, and the link is texted with the confirmation.

What to take from it. One 90 word notice, in plain words, placed where the person types their details, covers the form. The phone script reuses it, so the business keeps one source of truth.

The scenario is an illustration written for this page, not a real client or a decision of the regulator.

Checklist

  • List every place you collect personal information: web forms, phone, email, in person, photos, third-party lead lists.
  • Put a short notice at or before each point of collection, not only in the footer.
  • Cover each APP 5.2 matter that is reasonable in the circumstances, and write down why you left any out.
  • Name the countries in the notice if you disclose overseas and it is practicable.
  • Make the notice match the privacy policy (purposes, recipients, overseas disclosure).
  • For information collected from someone else, say so and say how it was collected.
  • Review when you add a tool or a new purpose.

Common traps

  • A privacy policy link in the footer only. APP 5 is about notifying at the time of collection.
  • Listing a purpose you do not have, or omitting a purpose you do (such as marketing).
  • Forgetting information collected indirectly, for example from a lead list or a referral partner.
  • Notice and policy disagreeing on overseas recipients.

Questions people ask

Is a collection notice the same as a privacy policy?

No. The privacy policy (APP 1) is the full document. The collection notice (APP 5) is the short statement given at or before the point of collection, which points to the policy.

Do I need the notice if the person already knows everything in it?

APP 5.1(b) allows you to 'otherwise ensure' the person is aware of the matters. Keep evidence of why you were satisfied they were.

When must the notice be given?

At or before collection, or if that is not practicable, as soon as practicable afterwards.
