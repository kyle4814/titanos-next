Twelve Blockers Behind A Green Suite

Over 2 and 3 November the product assistant went through four independent review rounds. Each round found a defect a buyer would have seen, 12 in total, and the tests were green before every one. The rule that came out of it: keep reviewing until a round comes back clean on a number path.

If you build, buy or sell anything with a chat assistant in it, this one is for you. It sells nothing and it costs nothing to read. This follows Issues 004, 005 and 006: the same unsolicited prototype, built from a building materials maker's public website, so the company is still not named. Nothing of theirs was touched.

What you will get out of it

  • How many rounds a reviewer needed before a green suite was believable, and what each round found.
  • The rule for when to stop reviewing and ship.
  • The exam numbers before and after.
  • What is still open.

Chapter 0. The number is 12

A blocking defect here means one a buyer would notice straight away: a wrong quantity, a wrong product, a confident yes where the answer is no. Each reviewer had built none of the code. Each time I re-ran the fixes myself instead of taking the builder's word for it, and each time the next reviewer found more.

Chapter 1. What each round found

  1. Round one: a stale product carried into a quantity answer, range names treated as aliases for single products, and a cheerful "yes, happy to help" for things the company does not sell.
  2. Round two: a product held from earlier hijacking a short question about a category, and the word "special" being read as a price question, among others.
  3. Round three: a bare "2400 x 1200" was read as an area and came back as 2,880,000 square metres and 480,000 bags. A tile size was also read as the job area.
  4. Round four: a tile size typed after a product card became the job area and went into the cart. A tile measurement in millimetres was read as a layer thickness, which turned a 10 square metre job into 285 bags. That one was already live in the version before this round.

A green suite tells you the problems you thought of are gone. It cannot tell you about the ones you did not.

Chapter 2. The rule

Keep reviewing until a round comes back with no blocking defect on a number path, then ship.

Not after one round, not after two. Numbers are where a wrong answer costs a buyer money, so numbers get the strictest exit test.

The limits of all of this

  • The last fix was checked by my own number probes, which found no wrong quantity. I have no fifth independent review round on record, so by my own rule it has not earned its pass yet.
  • It is live on two previews only, not merged to the main branch.
  • The exam I used to pick the better of two builds is partly used up, so 78 of 100 is a little flattering.
  • On the final exam, the clarifying-question category is still 6 of 14.
  • Quantity fallbacks and a fuzz suite for the quantity logic are still on the list, as are the earlier size, accessibility and performance misses from Issues 004 and 005.
  • It covers one maker's public catalogue, and whether any of it changes a purchase is UNKNOWN until they have seen it.
