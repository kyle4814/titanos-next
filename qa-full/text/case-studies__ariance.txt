The brief, in the client's words

Paraphrased from the owner's message. It is not a quote.

He wanted people to play with the ideas, not just read them. In his words, this stuff should be fun.
He wanted sliders, so a visitor can move a value and watch the picture change.
He wanted each person to have a profile, with levels and experience points, and several named labs inside it to try different things.
He wanted the open problem, the missing link between one level of the framework and the next, to be something a visitor can have a go at.
He wanted the diagrams to agree with the equations, with no picture that contradicts its own maths.
He wanted it to cost next to nothing to run, and to be ready to show an investor.
The challenge

Ariance is a framework with careful rules about what it does and does not claim. Some parts are plain mathematics. Some are an exact match to a known result, with the physical meaning left open. Some are proposals, some are the author's interpretation, and some are things that were tried and did not work.

That makes it hard to explain. A normal app would flatten those differences and make everything look equally settled. For a framework that depends on honest labelling, that would be the worst possible outcome.

So the job had two halves: make it playable and friendly, and keep every claim exactly as strong as the author says it is.

What we built
A game
A map of four zones, from the stable zero where nothing moves, up to the unrestricted climb. The first zone is a puzzle that teaches the framework's own rule by letting you try the wrong move first.
BUILT ON ITS OWN BRANCH, TESTED, NOT YET MERGED
Labs
Every profile can hold any number of named labs. A lab is a saved state that can be forked into a side branch, compared with another lab, and exported as a file or a shareable link.
MERGED AND TESTED
A Coupling Lab
A sandbox for the open problem. A visitor writes a candidate law in a small, safe expression language, and the app grades it against the framework's own constraints. It checks consistency. It never claims physical truth.
MERGED AND TESTED
Simulators
Six simulators drawn from the same functions that the tests check: the core curve, the refinement steps, a relativity match, a tunnelling match, a cup of water and a horizon slider.
BUILT ON ITS OWN BRANCH, TESTED, NOT YET MERGED
Four lenses
One system viewed four ways, so a visitor can see that the same object has several valid descriptions.
BUILT ON ITS OWN BRANCH, TESTED, NOT YET MERGED
Accounts, profiles and levels
Sign up, log in, experience points, levels and badges. A guest mode works with no account, and a guest profile can be claimed later. Each person can only see their own labs.
MERGED AND TESTED
Hosting that costs US$0 a month
The plan is a static site on a free hosting tier with a free database tier behind the accounts.

On the main branch, 128 of 128 tests passed across 7 files when we ran them at 12:40:50. Every formula in the maths engine, the exact matches to known results, the frozen region, the evaluation seam at zero, the safe expression parser, the Coupling Lab grader, the account adapters and the experience point rules. Two further branches report their own test counts and are not merged yet, so no combined total is claimed.

One label on every claim

Every concept, chart and sentence in the app carries exactly one of these labels, taken from the author's own standard. A candidate or an interpretation is never shown as established physics.

ESTABLISHED MATH
Follows from the stated definitions. Pure mathematics.
EXACT CORRESPONDENCE
An exact reparameterisation of a known result. Whether the identity is physical is open.
CANDIDATE
A proposed construction. Not derived.
INTERPRETIVE
The framework's reading. Not a physics result.
FAILED TEST
Tried, and it did not work. Kept on the record.
OPEN
Not settled.

The diagrams are drawn from the same functions the simulators use, so a picture cannot disagree with its own equation.

Consistency findings, offered first

While building, we checked the framework's own material against its own equations. We prepared the findings below for the author, as a gift and in plain terms, before asking anything of him.

The worked relativity example. Equation 3 gives a value of 0.7353 at a speed of 0.6c with k equal to 1. The exact match to the Lorentz factor gives 0.64. The mapping dS = gamma squared minus 1 reproduces 0.64 exactly, so we suggested updating the example or labelling it as illustrative.
A missing factor in one diagram. One panel writes the refinement step without its logarithm factor. Without that factor there is no frozen region, which would contradict the same panel's threshold graph and the formal operator.
A bound that should allow equality. A diagram states a strict inequality for the range of A, but also says A equals E squared when dS is zero. The bound should read less than or equal to, and a k of zero gives half of E squared for any dS above zero.
Two readings that are interpretive, not results. Two images show a breakout from the trap as if it were settled. By the author's own status table it is not established, because the current operator cannot leave the interval from zero to one. The app shows it as the open problem.
