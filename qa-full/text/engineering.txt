Method and sources
Model: Basic COCOMO, organic mode. Effort = 2.4 x KSLOC^1.05 person-months. Schedule = 2.5 x effort^0.38 months.
Size: KSLOC = 230.797, the non-blank, non-comment lines of production code across 12 repositories (mirrors, clones, worktrees and third-party code excluded), measured 9 October 2026. The window starts at the first commit on 4 May 2026 and runs 158 days, with commits on 66 of them.
Result: 727 person-months (60.6 engineer-years) over a 30.6-month schedule, which is about 23.8engineers. The earlier 44 engineer-years figure was the same model on 170,651 lines, measured 4 October.
Cost: AU$800 a day x 220 working days a year, the contractor rate from the Hudson Australia Cyber Security Salary Guide. 60.6 years x 220 days x AU$800 is about AU$11 million.
Per year: that output landed in 158 days, so a year at the same pace is 60.6 / (158 / 365) = about 140 engineer-years, about AU$25 million. The 10% and 2% lines are 10% and 2% of that. All of these are MODELLED, never revenue.
Compute: about US$30 (roughly AU$46) a month of AI tools while the main engine was built (to 29 September), and AU$300 a month since. Both are operator figures with no receipt on file.
Re-run it yourself: count the non-blank, non-comment lines in your own repositories, put the thousands into the two formulas above, and multiply person-months by your own day rate.

COCOMO estimates what a conventional team would take to write this much code. It is a model, not a timesheet, and it counts size, not value.
