Am I covered by the Privacy Act? Small business exemption checker

Most Australian small businesses are exempt from the Privacy Act, until they are not. Answer seven questions about your business and get a plain result, with the sources behind each question.

Written by Kyle Deligny, Brisbane. Last checked 28 November 2026 against the Act and OAIC guidance. General information, not legal advice.

Free checker, seven questions, nothing is stored or sent

Question 1 of 7

Was your business turnover more than AU$3 million in the last financial year (1 July to 30 June)?

Turnover means the total income your business earned in that year. The Act tests the previous financial year, and a business can drop in or out of the exemption as its turnover moves. Over AU$3 million, the Act covers you in full.

How the small business exemption works

The Privacy Act 1988 binds Australian Government agencies and "organisations". A business whose annual turnover is AU$3 million or less is a small business operator, and the Act does not treat it as an organisation. In practice that means the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs), including the duty to have a privacy policy, mostly do not apply to it. The test is set in section 6D of the Act and looks at the previous financial year, so a business can move in and out of the exemption as it grows or shrinks.

The exemption is about the Act, not about good practice. A small business still answers to its customers, to contracts that require privacy terms, and to other laws such as the Spam Act 2003 and state surveillance laws.

The exceptions that matter

A turnover under AU$3 million does not protect you if any of these apply:

  • Health service providers. A private sector business that provides a health service and holds health information is covered whatever its size. The definition is wide and takes in many allied health, wellness, counselling and gym style businesses.
  • Trading in personal information. Disclosing personal information to someone else for a benefit, service or advantage, or collecting it from someone else for one. Selling a customer list is the clear case.
  • Commonwealth contracted service providers. If you handle personal information to deliver services under a contract with an Australian Government agency, the Act applies to that work.
  • AML/CTF reporting entities. Businesses regulated under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 are covered for their AML/CTF activities.
  • Related companies. Group structures and related bodies corporate have their own rules in the Act, so a company inside a larger group should not assume it is exempt on its own turnover.
  • Special regimes. Tax file number handling, credit reporting, My Health Record, the Consumer Data Right, and businesses that have chosen to opt in to the Act.

The 2024 reforms and the 10 December 2026 start date

The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 received Royal Assent on 10 December 2024. It added new enforcement powers for the OAIC, a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy, and a new transparency duty for automated decisions. That duty starts on 10 December 2026: a business covered by the Act has to say in its privacy policy what kinds of personal information it uses in decisions made or substantially assisted by a computer program, where those decisions could significantly affect someone's rights or interests.

Two points for a small business. First, the automated decision duty is an APP duty, so it only reaches you if you are covered under the checker above. Second, the 2024 Act did not remove the AU$3 million exemption. Removal was agreed in principle by the Government in 2023 as a later stage of reform, and any change would have to pass Parliament. Check the current compilation of the Act on legislation.gov.au before relying on this page, because the law can move.

Questions people ask

Does the Privacy Act apply to small businesses?

Not automatically. A small business operator with annual turnover of AU$3 million or less is generally exempt from the Australian Privacy Principles. The exemption has exceptions, including health service providers, businesses that trade in personal information, Commonwealth contracted service providers and AML/CTF reporting entities.

What is the Privacy Act small business exemption threshold?

AU$3 million in annual turnover, tested against the previous financial year. It is set out in section 6D of the Privacy Act 1988.

Am I covered by the Privacy Act if I am a sole trader?

A sole trader is a small business operator if turnover is AU$3 million or less, so most are exempt. A sole trader who provides a health service, trades in personal information or does Commonwealth contract work is not protected by the exemption.

What changes on 10 December 2026?

Under the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024, businesses covered by the Act must describe in their privacy policy the kinds of personal information used in automated decisions that could significantly affect a person, and how those decisions are made. A small business that is exempt is not caught by this obligation.

Is this checker legal advice?

No. It is general information built on a simplified reading of the Act and the OAIC guidance. For a firm answer, contact the OAIC or a privacy lawyer.
